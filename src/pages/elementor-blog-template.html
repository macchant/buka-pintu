Ad Placement Logic

Count your H2 sections. For every 3 H2s, insert one ad slot after the 3rd section's content and before the 4th H2. A 1,500-word post with 5 H2s gets 1 ad. A 3,000-word post with 8 H2s gets 2 ads. Never put an ad immediately after a heading or inside a list.

What Are Incoterms and Why They Matter

Incoterms — short for International Commercial Terms — are a standardized set of rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC). They define who pays for freight, insurance, customs, and most importantly, when risk transfers from seller to buyer. The 2026 edition is the current version, updated to reflect modern trade practices.

Think of Incoterms as a universal language for international trade. When you and your supplier both agree on an Incoterm, there's no ambiguity about who's responsible for what. No surprises at the port. No arguments about who should have paid for the inspection.

⚠️
Incoterms Don't Cover Everything Incoterms only define transport obligations and risk transfer points. They don't cover payment terms, ownership transfer, or contract remedies. You still need a solid purchase agreement.

The 11 Incoterms 2026 Rules Explained

Incoterms 2026 includes 11 rules divided into two groups:

  • Rules for Any Mode: EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU, DDP — can be used for sea, air, rail, road, or multimodal shipping
  • Rules for Sea/Inland Waterway: FAS, FOB, CFR, CIF — specifically for sea freight where the bill of lading matters

FOB vs CIF: Which Saves You More

FOB (Free on Board) and CIF (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) are the two most commonly used Incoterms. Here's the critical difference: under FOB, you arrange and pay for freight and insurance yourself. Under CIF, the supplier arranges and pays for them — then passes the cost to you.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is the most commonly used Incoterm for sea freight?
FOB (Free on Board) is the most commonly used Incoterm for sea freight shipments from Asia. Under FOB, the buyer's responsibility and risk begin when the cargo is loaded onto the ship at the port of origin.
Does CIF mean the supplier is responsible for the cargo?
Partially. Under CIF, the supplier pays for freight and insurance to bring the cargo to the destination port. But risk transfers to the buyer once the cargo is loaded onto the ship at origin — not at destination.
Can I negotiate Incoterms with my supplier?
Absolutely. Incoterms are a starting point for negotiation, not a requirement. Most Chinese manufacturers quote FOB pricing by default, but you can negotiate CIF, DDP, or any other term that works better for your business.
What Incoterm should small importers use?
Most small importers starting out benefit from DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) because the supplier handles everything including customs clearance. As volume grows, switching to FOB gives you more control and typically lower costs.
What happens if I choose the wrong Incoterm?
Choosing the wrong Incoterm can result in surprise freight bills, customs delays, cargo being held at port, and disputes with suppliers over who should have covered specific costs. Getting it right from the start saves thousands.
